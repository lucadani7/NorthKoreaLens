# Editorial policy

This page explains how NK Lens chooses, labels and corrects what it publishes. If something we show does not follow these rules, please tell us.

## Where our content comes from

We use three kinds of sources, and every post shows which one it comes from:

- **Independent newsrooms** that report on North Korea with their own editorial standards.
- **Analysis**: research institutes and specialist outlets.
- **North Korean state media**, such as Rodong Sinmun. These are never presented as news.

The full list is on the [sources](/sources) page. We add a source only after checking that it is a real newsroom or research outlet with a track record.

## Articles from other sites

When we take something from another site:

- **We show** the headline, a short excerpt and the link to the original; for videos, the video itself, from YouTube.
- **We do not copy** whole articles or change their meaning.
- **We remove** from excerpts only advertising and self-promotion, such as links to Patreon or social media.
- **We sometimes add** context, always kept separate, in the note “What the article doesn't say”.

You can read the full story on the site that published it.

## How we handle propaganda

North Korean state media show how the regime wants to be seen, not what is really happening. That is why we publish them only like this:

- **Separately:** every post from state media sits in the Propaganda section and has a red border and label, wherever it appears.
- **Checked:** headlines from Rodong Sinmun come in as drafts and appear on the site only after the team has seen them.
- **Briefly:** we show only the headline and an excerpt, never whole articles.
- **With context:** when we can, the note “What the article doesn't say” adds what the original leaves out.
- **With a different question for AI:** your assistant is asked to explain the message and the propaganda techniques, not to take them as facts.
- **Without links in South Korea:** the details are below, under “For readers in South Korea”.

We publish propaganda with the label on it, so that you can recognise it elsewhere too.

## For readers in South Korea

South Korea blocks around 60 North Korean websites, including those of Rodong Sinmun and the Korean Central News Agency, under its Information and Communications Network Act. If you visit NK Lens from South Korea:

- you still see the headlines, excerpts and our notes on North Korean state media;
- we do not link to North Korean websites or show images hosted on them, and we tell you so where a link would be;
- links to every other site, such as independent newsrooms, work as usual.

We know your country only from your internet connection, which tells us the country and nothing more, and we do not store it.

Since 30 December 2025 the printed Rodong Sinmun can be read in South Korea without special permission, in libraries such as the North Korea Information Center at the National Library of Korea in Seoul. If the law on North Korean websites changes, we will update this rule.

## Our own posts

Posts written by the NK Lens team are marked as ours. We say where our information comes from and, when something is uncertain or unconfirmed, we say so plainly.

A note on German: if you read NK Lens in German, there are two words you will not find anywhere:

- **“Führer”**, because it calls to mind Adolf Hitler, the Nazi dictator. For the Kims we use “Machthaber” (“those who hold power”) instead, and the Leaders topic is called “Staatsführung” (“state leadership”).
- **“Anschluss”**, because it recalls the annexation of Austria by Nazi Germany in 1938. For Japan's annexation of Korea we write “Annexion”.

## Artificial intelligence

### How we use AI

- **Drafting:** the curiosities and their translations are written with the help of an AI assistant, starting from sources we have read. For the texts we write, we prefer Claude, by Anthropic; you can use any assistant you like (see below).
- **Checking:** a person reads every text before it appears on the site.
- **Sources:** they are listed under every text, so that you can check them too.
- **Exceptions:** we do not use AI on content from sources that do not allow it, such as the South Korean news agency Yonhap, and we say so on their posts.

### Ask your own AI assistant

On every post you can ask any AI assistant you like to explain the story. ChatGPT, Claude and Perplexity open directly, with the question already written. For any other, such as Gemini, Copilot, DeepSeek or Le Chat, you copy the question with one click and paste it into the chat. Everything happens in your own account: NK Lens sends nothing to the assistant and sees nothing of the conversation. We do not show these buttons on Yonhap posts.

We write only the first question, the one you see before you send it. Everything you ask after that, and what the assistant answers, is between you and the assistant, and is your responsibility, not NK Lens's.

### Why NK Lens has no AI of its own

Some news sites have built their own chatbot. We chose not to, for four reasons:

- **Your questions stay yours.** If NK Lens ran its own AI, every question you ask about North Korea would pass through our servers. With your own assistant, we see neither the questions nor the answers.
- **An answer in our name would have to be checked.** We put a source behind everything we publish. An AI can make mistakes, invent facts or be tricked into repeating propaganda, and an “NK Lens AI” would present all of that as ours. With your own assistant, it is clear that the answer comes from it, not from our team.
- **Every answer costs money.** We do not sell advertising or data about our readers, and we would rather spend what we have on our content.
- **You choose whom you trust.** You use the assistant you already know, in your language, and you see exactly what we ask it: the question is written out before you send it.

The downside: you leave NK Lens to read the answer, and its quality depends on the assistant you choose. We think that is a fair price for privacy and honesty.

## Protecting people

Some of the people who speak about North Korea put themselves or their families at risk. We do not publish details that could identify a defector or their relatives without their consent. Profiles on NK Lens can use a pseudonym, and we do not show readers' locations.

## Corrections

When we get something wrong, we correct it and say what we changed. If you spot a mistake, please tell us; a contact address will appear here soon.
