# About NK Lens

NK Lens is a news and community site about North Korea. We bring together reporting from independent newsrooms, analysis from researchers, and the official voice of the North Korean state, so you can follow what happens in the country and compare it with what Pyongyang says about itself.

## Why we built it

North Korea is one of the most closed countries in the world. Its citizens cannot read foreign news, and outsiders mostly see either official propaganda or scattered headlines. Good reporting exists, but it is spread across many sites and mostly in English or Korean.

NK Lens gathers it in one place, in thirteen languages, and puts state media next to independent reporting with clear labels. Seeing both side by side is often the fastest way to understand what is really going on.

## What you will find here

- **News** from independent newsrooms that report on North Korea, such as NK News and Daily NK.
- **Analysis** from researchers and specialist outlets.
- **Human rights** reporting, including the stories of people who left the country.
- **Curiosities** about history, language and everyday life, written by our team.
- **Propaganda**: headlines from North Korean state media, always labelled and shown with editorial context.
- **Discussions**, where readers can ask questions and share what they know.

## Who we are

NK Lens is an independent, non-commercial project. It is **not affiliated with the government of the DPRK** and does not support the regime in Pyongyang. It is not affiliated with any other government, party or company either.

We do not sell advertising and we do not sell data about our readers.

## The team

NK Lens is a one-person project for now. Read more about [the team](/team).

## How we work

We link to the original article for every story from another source and show only its title and a short excerpt. Our own posts are written by the team. The way we choose, label and correct content is described in our [editorial policy](/editorial-policy), and every outlet we use is listed on the [sources](/sources) page.
